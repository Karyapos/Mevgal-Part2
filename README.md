# Mevgal-Part2: Scenarios Analysis

## Project Overview

This second part of the **Mevgal** project expands the imaginary company created in [Part 1](https://github.com/Karyapos/Mevgal-part1) by evaluating four strategic business scenarios based on the original sales data of seven dairy products. Each scenario was modeled with predefined assumptions regarding sales behavior, logistics, production changes, and market effects. **SQL**, **Excel**, and **Power BI** were used to prepare the data, calculate scenario outcomes, and visualize the resulting performance metrics.

## Scenario 1 – Advertisement Investment (3 Years)

A one‑time advertisement investment was introduced to simulate increased market visibility.
Key assumptions included:

* Investment cost: 1.2 million

* Sales increases: +20% (Year 1), +15% (Year 2), +10% (Year 3)

* No changes to production, quality, or logistics

This scenario reflects a pure demand‑driven growth strategy based solely on marketing influence.

## Scenario 2 – Merchant Deal (1 Year)

A distribution agreement with five major merchants was modeled to absorb all surplus stock.
Key assumptions included:

* Merchants purchase excess units at ~0.50 above production cost

* No additional logistics required; spare truck capacity is used

* Public sales decrease by 10% due to market coverage by merchants

* No investment cost

This scenario evaluates the trade‑off between reduced market presence and improved utilization of unused production.

## Scenario 3 – Production Shift & Quality Reduction (3 Years)

A structural change in production was simulated by upgrading Unit A, removing Unit B since its facility is leased, and eventually lowering product quality.
Key assumptions included:

* Rental income from Unit B facility: 1.5M per year

* One‑time upgrade cost for Unit A: 1M

* Sales decreases due to quality reduction: 5%, 10%, and 15% over three years

This scenario examines the financial benefits of reduced production complexity against the long‑term impact of declining product quality.

## Scenario 4 – Logistics Upgrade (1 Year)

A logistics investment was modeled to reduce delivery constraints.
Key assumptions included:

* Investment cost: 1.4 million

* New trucks with 500‑unit capacity and multi‑temperature capability

* Updated trip costs per station: 110 / 130 / 145 / 110 / 175

This scenario measures the operational gains from increased capacity and temperature adaptability.

## Scenario Characteristics

The four scenarios were selected to represent different strategic directions commonly evaluated in business planning. The **advertisement scenario** reflects a conventional demand‑driven growth approach based on increased market visibility. The **merchant deal scenario** demonstrates an alternative sales‑channel strategy that prioritizes the utilization of surplus production rather than market expansion. The **production shift** scenario illustrates the trade‑off between simplified production operations and reduced product quality, a balance that is rarely considered but can arise in large‑scale manufacturing. The **logistics upgrade scenario** represents an operational improvement strategy aimed at increasing transport capacity and reducing long‑term distribution limitations.

## Conclusion

This second phase extended the project beyond operational modeling by incorporating strategic decision‑making into the simulated environment. The development of realistic business scenarios, grounded in market behavior and operational constraints, strengthened the overall understanding of strategic planning and scenario evaluation. The work also reinforced the ability to translate business assumptions into measurable data outcomes, to structure scenario logic with numerical consistency, and use **SQL**, **Excel**, and **Power BI** to simulate the operational and financial impact of different strategic choices.
